Pharmaceutical registration from pathway assessment to the registration certificate. We manage every stage of medicine registration on behalf of the manufacturer or marketing authorisation holder.

What we handle

One partner for every stage.

Pathway assessment

Medicines approved by WHO Listed Authorities or by Maturity Level 4 regulators, and WHO-prequalified medicines, may qualify for the 60-day recognition procedure. We check this first.

Dossier and submission

We prepare, validate and submit the full dossier in ICH CTD format, checked against the current requirements.

Laboratory testing

Sample delivery, customs clearance and supporting documents for the state laboratory.

Review and queries

We answer the authority's questions during primary and specialised review and address deficiencies within the deadlines.

Timeline

Five stages, each with a statutory period.

8–12 months
National procedure, in practice
5–6 months
Recognition procedure, in practice

The statutory review period is 210 days under the national procedure and 60 days under the recognition procedure or for WHO-prequalified products (Resolution No. 738). It excludes time to address deficiencies (up to 45 and 90 days), clinical studies (if required) and GMP inspection. In practice, national registration takes 8–12 months and recognition about 5–6 months. Where a GMP inspection is required, it drives the overall timeline — currently 9–12 months.

Up to 45 days are given to address deficiencies after the primary review and up to 90 days after the specialised review; these are not counted in the statutory period. Clinical studies (if required) and GMP inspection are also outside it. The certificate is issued within 2 working days of the decision and is valid for 5 years.

Recognition procedure. The recognition procedure covers medicines approved by WHO Listed Authorities or by regulators at Maturity Level 4 under the WHO Global Benchmarking Tool. WHO-prequalified medicines follow the same timeline. The statutory period is 60 days, with no laboratory testing or clinical studies.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions.

How do I register a drug in Uzbekistan?

Medicines and medical devices are registered by the State Institution "Pharmaceutical Products Safety Center" under the Ministry of Health. The five stages and their statutory periods are set out above.

How long does marketing authorization in Uzbekistan take?

The statutory review period is 210 days under the national procedure and 60 days under the recognition procedure or for WHO-prequalified products (Resolution No. 738). It excludes time to address deficiencies (up to 45 and 90 days), clinical studies (if required) and GMP inspection. In practice, national registration takes 8–12 months and recognition about 5–6 months. Where a GMP inspection is required, it drives the overall timeline — currently 9–12 months.

Which products qualify for the recognition procedure?

The recognition procedure covers medicines approved by WHO Listed Authorities or by regulators at Maturity Level 4 under the WHO Global Benchmarking Tool. WHO-prequalified medicines follow the same timeline. The statutory period is 60 days, with no laboratory testing or clinical studies.

Can I import medicines to Uzbekistan before registration?

Medicines must be registered before commercial import and sale. Without registration, medicines may be imported for research, preclinical and clinical studies, and for display at exhibitions. At the Ministry of Health's request, orphan drugs and medicines for highly dangerous or epidemic infections may be imported without registration; humanitarian aid requires a positive Ministry of Health conclusion.
